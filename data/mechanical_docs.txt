=== Engineering Mathematics ===
Linear Algebra focuses on vector spaces, matrix eigenvalues, and eigenvectors.
An Eigenvalue decomposition diagonalizes a square matrix to simplify linear systems.
Differential Equations model rate changes, solved using Laplace transforms.
Numerical Methods like the Runge-Kutta method solve ordinary differential equations iteratively.
Vector Calculus includes divergence, curl, and Green's and Stokes' theorems for surface integration.
=== Engineering Mechanics ===
Static equilibrium requires that the sum of forces and moments on a body equal zero.
Kinematics analyzes the motion of particles and rigid bodies without considering forces.
Newton's Laws describe particle kinetics, relating net force to mass and acceleration.
Friction forces oppose relative motion between surfaces, proportional to the normal force.
Virtual Work principle states that the virtual work done by active forces is zero for equilibrium.
=== Strength of Materials ===
Normal stress causes elongation or compression, while shear stress causes angular distortion.
Hooke's Law states that stress is linearly proportional to strain within the elastic limit.
Bending moment along a beam produces bending stress, maximum at the outer fibers.
Torsion of circular shafts creates shear stress that varies linearly from the center axis.
Euler Buckling occurs when long slender columns collapse under critical compressive loads.
Thin-walled pressure vessels experience hoop stress and longitudinal stress under internal pressure.
=== Theory of Machines ===
Kinematic links connect to form kinematic pairs, loops, and mechanical mechanisms.
Gears transfer rotational speed and torque, governed by the law of gearing.
Cams convert rotational motion into complex, pre-defined translational follower profiles.
Flywheels act as energy reservoirs, smoothing speed fluctuations due to torque cycles.
Gyroscopic couples occur when a spinning rotor's axis of rotation is forced to change direction.
=== Machine Design ===
Mechanical joints include threaded fasteners, welded connections, and riveted joints.
Shafts are designed based on torsional rigidity, bending strength, and fatigue limits.
Sliding contact bearings use hydrodynamic lubrication, while rolling contact bearings use balls or rollers.
Springs store mechanical energy, designed to avoid surging and buckling under compressive loads.
Gears design considers Lewis bending strength and Buckingham wear resistance criteria.
=== Fluid Mechanics ===
Fluid kinematics uses Eulerian and Lagrangian descriptions to model flow velocity fields.
Bernoulli's equation represents conservation of energy for steady, inviscid, incompressible flow.
Navier-Stokes equations express conservation of momentum for viscous Newtonian fluid flows.
Reynolds number dictates whether the flow regime is laminar or turbulent.
Boundary layer separation occurs when adverse pressure gradients slow fluid near walls.
=== Heat Transfer ===
Conduction transfers heat through material media, modeled by Fourier's law of conduction.
Convection describes heat transfer between surfaces and moving fluids, using Newton's law of cooling.
Radiation transfers energy via electromagnetic waves, governed by Stefan-Boltzmann's law.
Heat Exchangers transfer heat between fluids, analyzed using LMTD or NTU methods.
=== Mass Transfer ===
Fick's First Law describes steady-state mass diffusion, relating mass flux to concentration gradients.
Convective mass transfer models species transport between a boundary and fluid streams.
=== Thermodynamics ===
First Law of Thermodynamics represents conservation of energy, relating heat, work, and internal energy.
Second Law states that entropy in isolated systems always increases, preventing 100% heat conversion.
Carnot cycle defines the maximum theoretical efficiency limit for heat engines.
Pure substances undergo phase changes, modeled using thermodynamic property tables and diagrams.
=== Applied Thermodynamics ===
Rankine cycle models steam power plants using pumps, boilers, turbines, and condensers.
Brayton cycle represents gas turbine cycles, using isentropic compressors and turbines.
Otto cycle represents spark-ignition gasoline engines, utilizing constant-volume heat addition.
Diesel cycle represents compression-ignition engines, utilizing constant-pressure heat addition.
=== IC Engines ===
Engine performance parameters include indicated power, brake power, and thermal efficiency.
Knocking in spark-ignition engines is caused by auto-ignition of end-gas ahead of the flame front.
=== Power Plant Engineering ===
Steam power plants use reheaters and regenerators to increase thermodynamic Rankine efficiency.
Hydroelectric and nuclear power plants generate baseload electrical energy safely.
=== Refrigeration and Air Conditioning ===
Vapour compression refrigeration cycles use compressors, condensers, expansion valves, and evaporators.
Psychrometric charts plot moist air properties, including dry-bulb, wet-bulb, and dew-point temperatures.
=== Hydraulic Machines ===
Centrifugal pumps transfer energy to fluids by converting rotational kinetic energy to pressure.
Pelton, Francis, and Kaplan turbines extract kinetic and pressure energy from high-head water sources.
=== Manufacturing Engineering ===
Metal casting involves melting metal and pouring it into mold cavities for solidification.
Metal forming processes shape metals plastically using rolling, forging, extrusion, and drawing.
Metal joining includes arc welding, resistance welding, brazing, and soldering techniques.
=== Machining ===
Orthogonal cutting models tool geometries, shear angles, and chip thickness ratios.
Taylor's tool life equation relates cutting speed and tool wear duration mathematically.
=== Casting ===
Casting patterns account for shrinkage and draft allowances, using risers to prevent shrinkage cavities.
Casting defects include blowholes, cold shuts, hot tears, and shrinkage porosities.
=== Forming ===
Rolling reduces metal sheet thickness, while extrusion forces metal through dies for profiles.
Forging shapes metal using compressive hammer forces, enhancing grain flow and strength.
=== Metrology ===
Limits, fits, and tolerances define permissible dimensional limits for assembly interchangeability.
Linear and angular measurements use calipers, micrometers, slip gauges, and sine bars.
=== Industrial Engineering ===
Work study includes method study and work measurement to optimize production processes.
Plant layout design minimizes material handling costs and improves production throughput.
=== Operations Research ===
Linear Programming solves optimization problems using graphical or simplex algorithms.
PERT and CPM network techniques plan, schedule, and control complex projects.
=== Control Systems ===
PID controllers use proportional, integral, and derivative gains to reduce system error.
Bode plots and Routh-Hurwitz criteria evaluate feedback loop system stability.
=== Mechanical Vibrations ===
Single degree-of-freedom systems experience free, damped, or forced harmonic vibrations.
Vibration isolation reduces force transmission, while whirling critical speed causes shaft instability.
=== Materials Science ===
Crystal structures include BCC, FCC, and HCP packing alignments in metals.
Iron-carbon phase diagram shows steel phases, including ferrite, austenite, cementite, and pearlite.
=== Finite Element Analysis ===
Finite Element Method discretizes domains into finite elements linked by nodes.
Element stiffness equations relate nodal displacements to external applied forces.
=== CFD ===
Computational Fluid Dynamics discretizes conservation equations using finite volume methods.
Turbulence models like k-epsilon close the Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes equations.
=== Tribology ===
Tribology studies friction, wear, and lubrication regimes in sliding mechanical interfaces.
Journal bearings operate under hydrodynamic lubrication to support heavy rotating shafts.
=== Engineering Drawing ===
Orthographic projections display 3D objects in 2D plan, elevation, and side views.
Isometric projections draw objects with three axes spaced equally at 120 degrees.
